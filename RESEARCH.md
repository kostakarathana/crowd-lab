# Crowd Lab: evidence and model decisions

Research revision: 10 October 2026. Model: `crowd-lab-2.4`.

This is a research-informed, browser-based agent simulation. It is **not a reproduction or validated combination of the papers below**, and it is not a trained neural network. Each person has a route, local perception, a sampled movement planner, individual traits, companions, memory, and behavioral states. These decisions run locally, with no API keys or external AI service.

The review covers **31 research papers**, from foundational models through 2025 field observations, plus HSE operational guidance. Access was mixed: equations/methods/results were examined where accessible; abstract-only and indexed-excerpt sources are explicitly marked below. This is a focused implementation review, not a systematic review or a claim to have read every paper in full.

## What changed

1. **Anticipation before contact.** Predict potential collisions from relative velocities. Compare nine walking directions, keep a speed-dependent gap, and brake before reaching a queue. Physical contact is a separate layer: a person trying to stop can still be displaced by others.
2. **Urgency and cooperation are separate.** The former changes preferred speed, headway, and exit commitment. The latter changes personal space and willingness to push into a gap. At 90%+ the user-selected emergency regime additionally reduces braking and increases shoving, even for courteous agents; individual traits still vary. This is a scenario assumption, not a research-derived threshold. The old hard 90% cutoff for self-preservation was removed. Urgent people still mostly pursue exits; severe sustained compression can override that fixation.
3. **Individual exit decisions.** Compare walking distance with remembered visible queue conditions. Commit to a choice for a while; require a meaningful improvement before switching. Queue observations are wall-occluded and expire gradually. There is no automatic global knowledge of every exit queue.
4. **Companions.** Nearby pairs and trios share an initial exit preference, gently stay together, and briefly slow for a visible lagging partner. Cohesion weakens with urgency and is suspended in compression. They can separate; no invisible tether or permanent waiting.
5. **Varied response option.** An optional right-skewed response delay replaces simultaneous departure. This is separate from exact staged releases. It illustrates delayed response without pretending to model a specific alarm, hazard, or building population.
6. **Reproducible checks.** Tests cover anticipation, occlusion, group separation, exit memory, cooperation, response delays, containment, conservation, and evacuation completion. A three-seed benchmark suite compares this revision with the preceding model.

### Individual knowledge and timing

- A casualty no longer rebuilds everyone’s exit map, resets every exit-review timer or forces every waypoint refresh. Static exit/arrow maps describe known architecture. Only recognized, remembered immobile people become private route obstructions. Matching belief maps share a 64-entry cache, with at most two new maps per physics step; pending decisions use the architecture map plus immediate collision braking until a map is available.
- Independent seeded traits: attention 0.55–1, stress sensitivity 0.65–1.35, resilience 0.65–1.45, patience 0.6–1.4, adaptability 0.55–1.45, social trust 0.25–0.9, altruism 0–1, and a small continuous side preference. They are independent of age. These are model dimensions, not diagnoses, IQ scores, or population estimates.
- Perception runs on a personal 0.22–0.62 s base interval with 0.8–1.2 jitter, initialized at different phases. Recognition requires 0.18–1.08 s divided by attention, with continuing observations. Movement, aid and exit decisions are separate. Physical contact still acts at every 0.025 s integration step.
- Casualty vision ranges 5–8 m; a forward cone narrows under arousal. Nearby bodies within 1.2 m remain perceptible in all directions. Periodic glances examine all directions every 2–6 s. Walls block sight; standing bodies can occlude low casualties. At most 18–24 closest bodies receive recognition attention, while density snapshots can include up to 64 wall-visible neighbors. Occlusion is a 2D approximation, not eye-height optics.
- Up to eight casualties are remembered as observed positions/states for 8–20 s. Confidence decays, new visible information replaces old observations, and memories expire. People cannot track an unseen injured person’s new position. Helpers choose recognized nearby casualties; warning recipients receive concern only, never a hidden casualty’s location or identity.
- Local concern can relay within 3 m with wall checks, distance attenuation, half strength per hop, and time decay. Signals are snapshotted before each perception pass to prevent iteration-order cascades. No new event means relayed concern dies away instead of amplifying itself. This is an abstract warning cue, not an acoustics or language model.
- Current pressure/injury can raise a person’s stress directly. Witness memories and local concern have weaker, individually scaled effects. Fading stress, memory, route commitment and personal clocks prevent synchronized resets and arbitrary frame-to-frame randomness. Same seed and setup reproduce the same simulation.

Operational context: HSE guidance separates [arrival, circulation and departure controls](https://www.hse.gov.uk/event-safety/crowd-management-controls.htm), [monitoring/control within an event](https://www.hse.gov.uk/event-safety/crowd-management-control-inside.htm), and [hazard assessment](https://www.hse.gov.uk/event-safety/crowd-management-assess.htm). The sandbox supports geometry and release experiments; it does not replace those processes.

## Exact implementation and assumptions

### Local movement and physical motion

- Fixed integration step: 0.025 s. Movement decisions have independently sampled 0.12–0.28 s base intervals, multiplied by fresh seeded factors of 0.85–1.15. Response relaxation remains independently sampled in 0.4–0.6 s. Perception, aid, exit review, waiting and waypoint decisions each have their own clocks.
- Nominal free speed: `1.3 + 2.2 × urgency²` m/s, with seeded individual variation; injured walkers use 55%. The calm nominal value is consistent in scale with the walking speeds used in the literature, but the urgency curve is our choice.
- Nine headings relative to the route: 0, ±0.25, ±0.5, ±0.8, ±1.1 radians. Candidate cost combines deviation, turn continuity, reduced progress and predicted collision time. This is an original reduced hybrid, not the exact Moussaïd or Karamouzas algorithm.
- Perception reaches 3–6 m, bounded by speed and a base 1.7–2.4 s horizon, shortened by emergency drive. Bodies behind the agent are excluded from visual planning; walls occlude observations. At most 24 nearest visible bodies are considered for performance. Immediate collision avoidance remains a fast local reflex. Casualty recognition separately checks facing direction and intervening standing bodies.
- Headway starts at 0.75–1.15 s, modified continuously by urgency and courtesy. Motion is bounded by the gap to bodies ahead, with a small allowance for a moving leader. Competitive urgent agents retain up to 1.2 m/s desired drive into a queue; physical forces determine actual movement. Personal-space padding also grows after a compression experience.
- Adult disk radii remain 0.215–0.25 m; young children can be up to 25% smaller. Overlap contact stiffness, friction and wall repulsion remain illustrative. Pair forces cannot cross drawn walls. Hard wall containment remains; maximum speed/acceleration limits are numerical safeguards, not measured human limits.
- Headway affects **desired** motion. External pushing is not artificially zeroed when somebody waits, follows a guard or brakes. Downed bodies remain obstacles.

Model 2.4 retains the existing physical contact model but changes decision timing and perception at every urgency. At 90–100%, emergency drive is `E = (0.2 + 0.8 × (urgencyPercent − 90) / 10) × (1 − 0.35 × courtesy)`, or zero while seeking relief. Headway is multiplied by `1 − 0.65E`, personal-space padding by `1 − 0.85E`, anticipation horizon by `1 − 0.45E`, and collision cost by `1 − 0.8E`. Standing-body push allowance gains `3E` m/s (still capped by preferred speed). Near a visible standing person ahead within 0.85 m, forward acceleration gains up to `18E` model m/s², diminishing as forward speed reaches desired speed. This is an illustrative force scale, not measured human force. Existing equal/opposite contact forces transmit displacement and compression; contact scores are never assigned artificially. There is no extra drive when stopped, held by a guard, retreating, or braking for a fallen body. These coefficients and the 90% threshold are explicit sandbox choices requested for an extreme competitive emergency scenario.

### Exit decisions and groups

- A 0.5 m graph supplies walkable paths. Exit-specific fields assume knowledge of the floor plan and exits. This is **not** an unfamiliar-building search model. Temporary body obstacles enter an individual’s route map only after observation and recognition. The global body field remains for geometric safety checks and monitoring, not as a broadcast of casualty knowledge.
- On independent schedules, agents compare path travel time, stable seeded preferences, remembered queues and a small cue from nearby observed movement. Exit review intervals are `(1.8 + 2U + 2 × arousal) / adaptability` seconds. A door can be observed within 14 m only with a clear view; visible bodies within 4 m of it contribute to a rough delay estimate adjusted by width and speed. Estimates fade with a 15 s time constant.
- Commit for 5–12 s and require `(2 + 5 × urgency) / adaptability` seconds of estimated improvement before switching; urgency increases both. No fitted discrete-choice coefficients are claimed. Agents do not receive globally measured throughput or invisible queues.
- Legacy global congestion/caution fields remain for diagnostics and control-post routing. Individual waiting, sign-direction congestion and relief search use recent, wall-limited observations. Unknown areas are not assumed empty. These coarse estimates consider up to 64 nearby people and are still approximations; queue observation has a separate 14 m wall-limited door view.
- Group allocation pairs nearby people within 2.2 m, without moving their spawn positions. The target share is adjustable; sparse layouts may provide too few nearby partners. Members share initial exit bias, not telepathic live choices. Visible partners within 4 m get a bounded attraction; a lagging member can reduce pace to 80% for at most 8 s. Groups separate in contact, density above 3/m², or escape behavior.
- `Varied response` uses `min(40, 0.5 − log(U) × (3 + 9 × (1 − urgency)))` seconds. The delay is right-skewed but uncalibrated. Exact staged-release schedules are unchanged. People already occupy the room; these are departure delays, not incoming arrivals.

### Compression, control and health

- Urgency continuously suppresses voluntary retreat; a minority remain responsive at maximum urgency. Severe accumulated distress can trigger relief-seeking for anyone. There is no hard 90% cutoff for self-preservation; the separate emergency shoving regime begins at 90%. A retreat, once started, can finish before exit-seeking resumes.
- Agents seek reachable lower-density space, avoid a denser band en route, regroup briefly and retain slowly fading caution. Thresholds are designed behavior, not measured probabilities of panic or self-preservation.
- Guards retain wall-limited local metering, upstream-pressure relief, bounded six-second holds and shared capacity. They cannot freeze bodies against physical compression. Capacity and compliance need real-world calibration; communication quality, visibility over heads and guard movement are omitted.
- Arrows retain the user-requested 85% encounter response and approximately equal odds at equal density. Their red 4.5 m radius is wall-clipped. The setting is not an empirical sign-detection rate. Agents do not walk back to a sign tip they have passed. Progress renews commitment, short queues do not cancel it, and unresolved jams time out after up to 60 s. On completing an arrow, choose a reachable exit in the indicated direction and commit for 20 s; ignore immediately opposing signs for 8 s. These rules prevent repeated reversals while preserving the initial density-weighted split between conflicting signs.
- Contact is a normalized overlap-force proxy; local density uses a 1 m disk and is biased near walls. Neither is a validated safety threshold. Falls, recovery, injury and death still use invented exposure thresholds. They do not estimate clinical outcomes; no research in this review calibrates them.

### Age variation and individual vulnerability

`Age variation` defaults to 35%. At 0%, all agents are adults aged 18–64 and their new physical multipliers are exactly 1. At 100%, the expected mix is 25% children aged 6–17, 25% older adults aged 65–85 and 50% adults. Cohort shares scale linearly with the slider. This is an adjustable illustrative mix, not census data. A separate seeded random stream preserves spawn positions and courtesy when the setting changes. Children are visibly smaller.

With youth `Y = (18 − age)/12` for children (otherwise 0), aging `O = (age − 55)/30` for older adults (otherwise 0), and individual variation `V = (U − 0.5) × mix`, speed multiplier is `clamp(1 − 0.18Y − 0.35O + 0.16V, 0.55, 1.1)`, strength is `clamp(1 − 0.5Y − 0.45O + 0.24V, 0.4, 1.15)`, and contact-dose susceptibility is `clamp(1 + 0.9Y + 0.85O − 0.3V, 0.85, 2.1)`. Relative mass is `clamp(1 − 0.55Y + 0.24 × (independent U − 0.5) × mix, 0.4, 1.15)`. Strength scales voluntary acceleration and resistance to being moved; mass divides external contact acceleration. Body radius scales by `1 − 0.25Y`. These choices are intentionally heterogeneous, but **not calibrated biological or medical models**. Safe conditions do not produce age-related injuries; higher susceptibility only accelerates existing compression exposure. Disabling casualties still disables all casualty transitions.

Supplemental age evidence (abstracts/primary summaries reviewed): [Najmanová & Ronchi (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11430) found age-dependent movement in preschool drills, with strong differences between corridors, stairs and doors; this does not justify treating every child as uniformly slow. [Li et al. (2018 preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.11460) highlights body size and motivation in child bottleneck flows. [Kuligowski et al. (2012), NIST](https://www.nist.gov/publications/evacuation-people-disabilities-stairs) reports heterogeneous assisted-living evacuation speeds on stairs; these are not transferred as flat-floor speeds here. None of these sources supplies the implemented strength or injury multipliers.

### Helping and personal stress

Cooperation also controls attempts to aid visible casualties within 3.5 m. An independent altruism draw is compared with `courtesy × (1 − 0.9 × personalUrgency)²`; zero cooperation disables aid. At most two mobile helpers approach a casualty. Helpers must be released, uninjured, below contact 0.2 and density 3/m², with a clear reachable approach. They pause beside downed people or briefly accompany injured walkers. Assistance lasts 4–7 s, with a 7–14 s helper cooldown and 45 s per-target memory. A dead person receives a brief check (1–2 s); each helper remembers that check permanently for the run. Dead people never revive.

Within body radii plus 0.55 m, helpers multiply the existing recovery hazard by `1 + 0.75 × min(2, helpers)`. The original two seconds of relief, low density and physical room to stand are still required. Helpers do not carry people or guarantee recovery. Compression makes them abandon aid; high personal urgency sharply reduces willingness. Only recognized casualties qualify for aid. New aid choices occur on individual 0.4–1.2 s schedules. Existing assignments reserve at most two helpers and physical danger cancels them immediately. See the existing Drury et al. source for evidence that helping occurs; these exact capacities, durations and recovery effects are scenario rules, not measured rescue efficacy.

Every person has a separate arousal value from 0 to 1. Compression above contact 0.25, personal injury/falling, or visible casualties raises it; walls block casualty awareness. Threat is the maximum of normalized compression, personal injury (1), remembered casualty confidence × 0.6, and local relayed concern × 0.4. Target arousal is `clamp(threat × sensitivity × appraisal, 0, 1)`, where appraisal is 1 for current compression/injury and 0.78 otherwise. It rises at `(1 + threat) / resilience` per second and falls by `0.025 × resilience` per second. Witnessing distress does not automatically push everyone into the same emergency state. Personal urgency is `baseUrgency + (100 − baseUrgency) × arousal`, so the user's slider remains the baseline. Strong compression/injury can reach emergency shoving within seconds. Exposure weakens queue comparisons and voluntary waiting, increases exit commitment and preferred speed, reduces helping, and lowers nearby guard capacity. Severe distress can still trigger self-preservation. This is the requested stress response, not a universal claim that real emergencies eliminate rational thought.

## Venue design normalization (model 2.3)

After a layout edit/reset, a static navigation flood from the exits determines usable floor. Spawn cells are restricted to reachable space, redistributing the requested crowd there up to the available non-overlapping spawn capacity. An upstream concourse with no room to spawn can use reachable downstream floor. No usable exit means an empty editable draft, not trapped people.

Wall drawing snaps endpoints to nearby boundaries. Walls are split at exact segment intersections (or nearby endpoint projections for imported gaps too narrow for a body). Pieces with no reachable side are removed; pieces with one reachable side are exterior boundaries. Both-sided pieces remain internal barriers. The original straight/diagonal geometry is retained, with reachability evaluated using the existing 0.5 m navigation grid and body clearance. Sealed floor is shaded out using that grid. Hidden guards/signs are removed. Undo retains the prior layout; erased boundaries and new exits recompute usable floor. Empty drafts keep their drawing geometry so they can be repaired.

This happens only at setup. Temporary obstruction by casualties never teleports agents or erases the venue during a run. Regions too narrow for the navigation clearance count as solid. Existing exits remain on the original venue edges. This is an editor rule, not a change to human behavior.

## Verification and limits

Run `node --test tests/*.test.js`. Run `node benchmarks/run.js ./engine.js benchmarks/results.json` for 12 scenarios: three seeds × calm, urgent/cooperative, urgent/competitive, and wider exit. Each has 200 people in the concert geometry, no companions, age variation 0%, no casualties and a 180 s time limit. Central flow counts the middle 80% of exits (160 people) divided by its discharge interval. `benchmarks/baseline.json` was generated from commit `95c9a53`, using the same harness; that engine ignores the new cooperation setting.

These are **synthetic regression and plausibility checks**, not validation against measured trajectories. Numerical agreement with a published flow range would not establish validity for different geometry, population, hazard or density. `benchmarks/RESULTS.md` records observed results and practical limits.

Not implemented: smoke/fire/hazard fields, stairs, wheelchair-specific mobility, body rotation/ellipses, lifting/carrying or clinical rescue, semantic conversations (only attenuated local concern is modeled), unfamiliar-venue exploration, wall failure, validated human contact forces, empirically fitted casualty risk, or the 2025 chiral continuum model. The app remains an exploratory sandbox, not venue-design or evacuation-certification software.
